# Browser verification

Read when finishing or modifying the deck. Use available browser tooling and record observations rather than assuming that source code or a static screenshot proves the animation works.

1. Open the actual deliverable. Check every slide and semantic step after motion settles at the target presentation size: text is legible, visual hierarchy is intact, no content clips or overlaps, and images load. Inspect at least one phone viewport; a fixed 16:9 stage may become small in portrait, so note that limitation rather than claiming responsive reflow.
2. For each representative step, inspect initial, middle and final states, then watch actual playback. Check moving paths, endpoints and rules against visible glyphs; bounding-box or scroll checks alone can miss a line crossing a subtitle. Compare the last animated frame with the first frame after cleanup: typography, line breaks and geometry must not jump. Pause/seek tooling helps inspection but does not replace watching the motion.
3. Observe each authored page-transition type at its midpoint and completion, including reverse travel. Check visibility, stacking and shared-object continuity; only the active page should receive focus or interaction. Use the step and direct-page controls, rapid reversals, page jumps and replay. A back-step must restore a readable stable result and a subsequent forward trigger must replay correctly. Test delivered timers, dialogs and other interactions rather than confirming that their buttons exist.
4. Pause during a beat and verify both the beat and environment freeze. Switch the document to the background and return; offscreen loops must stop and stale callbacks must not change the restored page. Exercise reduced motion on entry and while a beat is running: paint the current step's stable meaning, retain navigation to later steps, and stop large movement. Inspect runtime/console and failed asset requests if tooling allows.
5. When WebGL is used, explicitly disable it or simulate context loss and inspect the fallback. Preserve the scene's focal composition and readable content, rather than accepting an empty background or missing main object. If the deck offers original-image zoom, open and close it, retain the full source image and caption, and check that modal controls do not advance the talk. Illustrative evidence must remain labelled as such.
6. When offline or self-contained behavior is promised, inspect dependencies and open without network access if feasible. Check the folder actually delivered, including local libraries and licenses. If unavailable, explain what was verified and what was not. Exercise offered export hooks: default whole-page export should reveal the complete final content; an optional step export should preserve that step. Wait after export and confirm Canvas, WebGL and DOM stay deterministic without ambient drift. Check notes, editable text and other format constraints only when offered.
7. Verify the design decision and motion plan against rendered output: identify how the selected recipe, primary reference, or custom design system shaped composition and the graphic vocabulary, whether the opening/closing have a deliberate relationship, and whether explanatory scenes move differently for a reason. Review a continuous 3–5-page passage when authorized scope permits: variation in scale, content density, movement and reading holds must support the story. Do not generate extra pages to satisfy this check while a smaller proof is awaiting confirmation. A resource-read log, zero overflow, or many named animations does not prove quality. Compare with an available reference on the same content without treating one run as a general win rate.

For a representative proof, verify and present only the agreed pages and real motion; record the user direction decision separately from technical results. Do not expand the deck while an explicit direction confirmation is pending.

Fix issues and repeat the affected checks. Deliver the file path or runnable URL, navigation instructions, and concise facts about what was checked; avoid reporting unobserved performance or exports as successful.
